Identifying the Gap

The First Nations Health Authority (FNHA) supports access to mental health services for eligible First Nations people across British Columbia through a directory of over 2,400 registered providers. However, the existing directory relied on PDF-based information and Google Maps with overlapping markers, making it difficult to search, filter, and compare providers.

Beyond usability, accessing mental health care also involves challenges related to awareness, accessibility, and trust. Research highlighted barriers such as limited internet connectivity in remote communities, unfamiliar clinical designations, and experiences of discrimination within healthcare settings.

These findings showed that improving the search experience alone would not be enough. The redesigned directory also needed to prioritize clarity, accessibility, and culturally supportive information to help users make informed decisions.

"How might we help First Nations people quickly discover and confidently choose mental health providers that match their needs, while reducing cognitive load and creating an intuitive, culturally supportive search experience?"

Understanding & Validating

Research Approach & Insights

Our team conducted secondary research to understand FNHA's services, mental health access barriers, digital accessibility, and cultural safety considerations. Research topics were divided among team members to build a shared understanding of the challenges from different perspectives.

We also gathered insights through stakeholder interviews to better understand existing workflows, user needs, and accessibility barriers. Based on our findings, we developed four personas representing different needs and perspectives, including Elders, youth, LGBTQ2SIA+ community members, and people with colour vision deficiencies.

These personas helped guide design decisions around navigation, readability, inclusive language, and culturally supportive experiences.

What research surfaced:
  • Accessibility: Limited connectivity and varying levels of digital literacy highlighted the importance of a lightweight, easy-to-navigate interface.
  • Clarity: Unfamiliar provider designations and complex information made it difficult for users to identify suitable services.
  • Awareness: Some people were unfamiliar with the available mental health benefits and how to access them.
  • Cultural Safety: Trust, representation, and culturally relevant information were important considerations when searching for care.

Together, these findings informed our approach to creating a more accessible and intuitive provider directory.

User Testing & Iterations

01

Internal Testing

Format

We conducted task-based usability testing with 22 participants at the Centre for Digital Media, comparing a Figma prototype against a Lovable build side by side.

What we found

Participants found filters helpful when searching for suitable providers, with 86% rating them as helpful and 96% identifying availability as important. While 64% found the map useful, feedback highlighted challenges with overlapping markers, information-heavy provider cards, and the clarity of certain interface elements.

What changed

Based on the feedback, we explored ways to simplify provider information, improve visual hierarchy, and make key filters and availability information easier to understand.

02

External Testing

Format

We conducted a second round of usability testing with approximately 35 participants to evaluate the revised experience and identify remaining usability issues.

What we found

Participants sometimes entered general search terms or searched in the wrong field. They also encountered difficulties with no-result states, mobile readability, filter interactions, and understanding certain interface elements.

What changed

We simplified the landing page, focused the main search on cities, refined the filter organization, and improved the visibility of important actions. We also prioritized displaying providers by availability and reduced the emphasis on the map in the final directory experience.

Designing the Solution

Wireframes & Features

Before moving into wireframing, our team explored different approaches, including improving the existing Google Maps experience and developing a standalone platform. We moved forward with a standalone web platform to provide greater flexibility over the interface, search experience, and provider information.

We explored several design directions, including search improvements, map interactions, accessibility, cultural safety, and mobile-first design. These ideas were refined based on research findings, project scope, and usability testing.

As one of two UX/UI designers, I focused on designing and refining desktop wireframes while collaborating with the other designer on a consistent design system, including colours, typography, iconography, and reusable UI components. I also supported design handoff and reviewed the implementation to help maintain consistency between the designs and the developed product.

Figma File Live Site
Feature 01

Search & Filter

Users can search for mental health providers by city and apply filters based on their needs, including service type, virtual care, and cultural preferences. Search results are organized by availability, allowing users to identify the quickest available options without manually comparing providers.

Feature 02

Provider Profiles & Saved Lists

Provider profiles present essential information, including services, availability, contact details, and referral requirements. Users can save providers for later and report incorrect or outdated information to help maintain the directory's accuracy.

Feature 03

Admin Portal

We also developed an admin portal to help FNHA staff manage provider information more efficiently. The portal includes a dashboard, provider management tools, and a report review system for handling user-submitted corrections, supporting more accurate and up-to-date directory information.

Learning & Growth

Working on this project strengthened my understanding of user-centred design, accessibility, and collaboration within a multidisciplinary team. As one of two UX/UI designers, I contributed to research, personas, desktop wireframes, the design system, and design handoff.

Key Learnings

  • Designing with users in mind: With limited direct access to our target audience, we relied on research and personas to guide our decisions. This experience reinforced the importance of involving real users earlier in the design process.
  • Being open to change: Usability testing challenged our initial desktop-focused approach and highlighted the importance of mobile accessibility. It reminded me to make design decisions based on user behaviour rather than assumptions.
  • Balancing ideas with project scope: Working within a limited timeline taught me to prioritize features based on user needs, technical feasibility, and the project's goals.
  • Collaborating beyond design: Working closely with developers helped me understand the importance of clear communication, consistent design documentation, and reviewing the implementation throughout development.

Looking back, I would have liked more opportunities to conduct direct research with our target users earlier in the project. Although our research and testing helped inform the final design, earlier engagement could have provided deeper insights into their needs and challenges.

This project also showed me that designing a digital experience goes beyond the user-facing interface. Supporting FNHA staff in maintaining accurate provider information was equally important to making the directory useful over time.
