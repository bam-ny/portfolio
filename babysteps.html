BabySteps

Designing an immersive AR experience for emotional bonding during pregnancy

Role
UX/UI Designer
Tool
Figma
Project Client
Tandem Impact Collective
(Client-Based Academic Project)
Duration
Jan 2026 – Apr 2026 (12 Weeks)

BabySteps is an AR-based prenatal experience designed to support emotional bonding between expectant parents and their developing baby. Rather than tracking statistics, it turns each week of pregnancy into an immersive night sky that evolves alongside the baby's growth, transforming fetal development into something parents can witness, explore, and return to.

The goal was to design an experience that feels intuitive, comforting, and emotionally resonant, one that includes partners as active participants, not bystanders, while still grounding every moment in real developmental milestones.

Identifying the Gap

Expectant parents — especially in the early stages of pregnancy — often struggle to feel connected to their developing baby. While existing prenatal tools focus on delivering information, they rarely create emotionally engaging or meaningful experiences.

As a result, the baby's development can feel abstract and distant, making it difficult for parents to form a strong emotional connection before birth.

This gap is even more pronounced for partners, who are often excluded from the experience due to tools centered primarily on the gestational parent. With limited opportunities to actively participate, co-parents can feel disconnected from the bonding journey.

Without intentional, engaging moments that make development feel tangible, opportunities for connection are often missed.

"How might we create meaningful and emotionally engaging experiences that help parents feel connected to their baby throughout pregnancy — while enabling partners to actively participate in the journey?"

Understanding & Validating
01

Research

We started with user interviews with expectant parents to understand how bonding actually forms — and where it breaks down. Research surfaced a consistent need for interactions that feel calming and judgment-free: no guilt for missing a day, no clinical framing, no pressure to feel a certain way. We also mapped fetal development week-by-week and audited existing prenatal apps — almost all deliver facts, none deliver feeling.

02

Concept Lockdown

We explored three directions: a Scrapbook for memory-keeping, a Bookcase for ambient daily reflection, and a Constellation concept expressing development through celestial storytelling. We merged the strongest elements into one unified direction — the Night Sky — and locked scope to six narrative phases plus a layer of daily rituals.

03

User Testing & Insights

We validated across two rounds: an internal Figma prototype test with 42 participants, and a live AR prototype test with 65 external participants. Testing confirmed the emotional core was working, but also surfaced real friction — holding a phone overhead was physically tiring, and the 3D baby model read as too clinical ("alien-like," "sterile").

04

Iterations

Each round of feedback translated directly into changes: AR content was repositioned lower and constrained to roughly 180° for physical comfort; the baby model's transparency was reduced and organs hidden by default; pinch-to-zoom was added; and onboarding was rewritten to name the Night Sky concept upfront.

Designing the Solution

Designing a Meaningful Pregnancy Journey

From first interaction to ongoing engagement, the experience is structured to guide users through each stage of pregnancy with clarity and emotional depth. The Night Sky became the emotional center of BabySteps — a world-space AR dome anchored in the parent's own home that evolves from deep night to sunrise across six narrative phases, mirroring the real developmental journey from Week 1 through birth. Around that core, we built two supporting systems: a 3D visualization that makes development tangible without feeling medical, and a Journey & Daily Log that gives both parents a low-pressure way to participate.

Figma File
Feature 01

AR Night Sky Experience

Purpose

To create an immersive environment that represents the baby's journey over time.

What we designed

  • A calming night sky as the main interaction space, built in three layers — background, midground, and foreground — so the sky shifts gently with each visit without ever feeling repetitive
  • Storytelling moments triggered by visual cues, tied to real developmental milestones like the first heartbeat
  • Interactive elements such as adding memories to the sky, turning the dome into something personal rather than decorative
Feature 02

3D Baby Visualization

Purpose

To make fetal development feel tangible and emotionally resonant, without tipping into clinical.

What we designed

  • An interactive 3D model parents can rotate, zoom, and explore at their current stage of pregnancy
  • Milestone-touch interactions — tapping to trigger a heartbeat or first movement
  • A warm, soft rendering style with organs hidden by default behind an optional toggle, refined directly in response to testers describing early versions as "alien-like" and "sterile"
Feature 03

Journey & Daily Log

Purpose

To give parents — and partners — a shared, low-pressure way to document and show up for the experience.

What we designed

  • Optional daily rituals, like leaving a message for the baby, placing a memory star, or taking a quiet breathing moment
  • Memory capture through photos, voice memos, or text, guided by gentle (never mandatory) prompts
  • A Partner View so co-parents can witness and contribute to the same sky, with no streaks and no guilt for missing a day

Learnings & Growth

This project challenged me to design beyond functionality — for emotion, storytelling, and user comfort in a space where getting the tone wrong carries real weight.

  • Balance immersive experiences with usability — our first AR builds asked users to hold their phones overhead; what felt magical in week one became a comfort problem by week nine, especially for heavily pregnant users.
  • Design for sensitive, emotional contexts — habit-app conventions like streaks and reminders read as guilt-inducing here, so every feature had to ask: does this feel like support, or surveillance?
  • Translate abstract concepts into clear interactions — the Night Sky metaphor made invisible biology tangible, but testing showed symbolism alone wasn't enough without clear onboarding to anchor what it meant.
  • Iterate on real user feedback — hearing our 3D baby model called "alien-like" was hard to hear, but it was the clearest brief we got all semester: reduce transparency, hide organs by default, keep what worked.

Beyond the specific lessons, BabySteps reshaped how I think about my role as a designer — not just solving usability problems, but holding the emotional stakes of the people on the other side of the screen.
